# Megakernels across GPUs and accelerators: workload, hardware, and software landscape

Research snapshot: **2026-09-29**. This is a broad survey of public papers, project repositories, and vendor documentation available on that date, with emphasis on primary sources. Performance figures below are **authors' reported results**, not measurements reproduced in this repository. The term *megakernel* is used inconsistently: here it means one accelerator dispatch that executes several dependent stages of an application, often through resident workers and an in-kernel schedule. A **persistent kernel** keeps workers resident while they process multiple tasks; a persistent GEMM is not necessarily a megakernel. A **CUDA Graph** replays a graph of launches with lower host overhead but normally retains its individual kernel boundaries. [CUDA Graphs](https://docs.nvidia.com/cuda/cuda-programming-guide/04-special-topics/cuda-graphs.html), [Triton persistent-kernel tutorial](https://triton-lang.org/main/getting-started/tutorials/gluon/persistence.html), [Mirage Persistent Kernel (MPK)](https://github.com/mirage-project/mirage).

The referenced local TIRx and reproduction paths describe workspace-only experiments; they are not included in this document-only publication. Public paper, project, and vendor links are the sources for the landscape claims. The separate [MegaBench agent suite](megabench/README.md) evaluates agent-written kernels rather than those local implementations.

## Main findings

- **No workload intrinsically needs a megakernel.** It is most useful when many short, dependent GPU operations spend material time in dispatch gaps, full-grid boundaries, repeated intermediate-memory traffic, or exposed communication, and when one resident schedule can keep enough useful work in flight. Hazy Research measured this directly for batch-one Llama-1B decode; its H100 megakernel reached 78% of memory bandwidth, whereas its vLLM/SGLang baselines reached at most 50% in that test. [Hazy Research](https://hazyresearch.stanford.edu/blog/2025-05-27-no-bubbles).
- **The strongest evidence is for autoregressive inference and MoE layers.** Whole-model decode, routed/quantized MoE, tensor-parallel inference, and expert-parallel training have published implementations. Iterative scientific kernels and simulation also have credible persistent/fused-kernel evidence. Graphics provides an important counterexample: complex path tracing can run faster as separate wavefront stages because a monolithic shader causes divergence and register pressure. [MPK paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.22219), [MonoMoE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04244), [UniEP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.19241), [PERKS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.02064), [Laine et al.](https://research.nvidia.com/sites/default/files/pubs/2013-07_Megakernels-Considered-Harmful/laine2013hpg_paper.pdf).
- **H100/B200 are common research targets, not universal requirements.** Reported implementations also run on NVIDIA L20, RTX 3090, and AMD MI300X. The actual floor is set by the selected implementation's ISA, available GPU memory, resident-block/resource limits, and, for distributed work, peer-accessible interconnect and communication runtime. [Ada-MK](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.11581), [Luce](https://github.com/PixelML/luce-megakernel), [Kog AI](https://blog.kog.ai/building-a-single-kernel-latency-optimized-llm-inference-engine-on-amd-mi300x-gpus/).
- **Agent-generated megakernels are feasible but harness-dependent.** [ForgeMegakernel](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.12379) uses staged agent edits plus an independent intermediate-state oracle; open-source [AutoMegaKernel](https://github.com/RightNow-AI/AutoMegaKernel) restricts agent changes behind a statically checked schedule IR. The key lesson from [KernelBench-Mega](https://github.com/Infatoshi/kernelbench.com/blob/master/benchmarks/mega/DEVLOG.md) is to verify that a purported single kernel is not merely a CUDA Graph replay of many kernels. See the [agentic-generation survey](#ai-agent-approaches-to-writing-megakernels-2026-09-29-update).

## Workloads: where the evidence is

| Workload | Why one resident execution can help | Public evidence and boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Low-batch dense LLM decode** (especially batch 1, small-to-mid models) | Many short norm, GEMV, attention, activation, and reduction stages; avoid launch gaps and pipeline weight loads across stages. | [FlashFormer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22758) demonstrates whole-model, low-batch inference. [Hazy Llama-1B](https://hazyresearch.stanford.edu/blog/2025-05-27-no-bubbles) reports >1.5× vs SGLang on H100 and B200 for its test; [Ada-MK](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.11581) reports up to 23.6% vs TensorRT-LLM on L20. Gains depend strongly on model, batch, context, precision, and baseline. |
| **Multiple autoregressive tokens and sampling on device** | A persistent loop avoids host intervention between greedy/sampling steps; in-kernel token selection can feed the next embedding lookup. | This repository's [Qwen3 TIRx kernel](sandbox/tirx/megakernel_qwen3.py) generates `N` greedy tokens in one launch. [Kog AI's MI300X monokernel](https://blog.kog.ai/building-a-single-kernel-latency-optimized-llm-inference-engine-on-amd-mi300x-gpus/) includes sampling. Some other “whole-model” systems fuse one decode **step**, not an entire generated sequence, so compare dispatch boundaries carefully. |
| **Hybrid attention/recurrent decode** | Many heterogeneous short stages and recurrent state updates can be kept in one schedule. | [Luce](https://github.com/PixelML/luce-megakernel) reports a batch-one, BF16 Qwen3.5-0.8B decode megakernel on RTX 3090 (413 vs 267 tokens/s against its llama.cpp baseline). Its **prefill is separate** (`prefill.cu`), even though its benchmark reports prefill numbers. |
| **Single-GPU routed or quantized MoE decode** | Routing creates data-dependent expert work; fuse top-k, quantization, both projections, activation, and reduction while avoiding tiny expert grids and padding. | [MonoMoE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04244) reports up to 1.54× for the complete routed-MoE operator vs vLLM Triton Grouped GEMM and up to 18.7% lower end-to-end time/token on H200. [Cohere's serving engine](https://github.com/cohere-ai/cohere-megakernel) includes MoE in a full decode pass. This repository also has [Qwen3-MoE](sandbox/tirx/megakernel_moe.py) and [GPT-OSS](sandbox/tirx/megakernel_gptoss.py) TIRx paths. |
| **Distributed MoE inference / expert parallelism** | Dispatch, expert compute, combine, and inter-GPU transfer can overlap at tile granularity instead of waiting at phase boundaries. | [FlashMoE](https://github.com/osayamenja/FlashMoE) implements a one-kernel distributed MoE layer with NVSHMEM. [FlashInfer MegaMoE](https://flashinfer.ai/2026/09/22/mega-moe.html) offers fused expert-parallel backends. [Weave](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21483) dynamically partitions SMs between communication and computation; it reports 1.33× end-to-end on four H100 SXM GPUs. These are generally **MoE-layer** megakernels, not a whole LLM. |
| **Tensor-parallel, larger-batch LLM inference** | Concurrent compute, HBM traffic, attention, and NVLink traffic can occupy different SM resources at the same time. | [Hazy's Llama-70B megakernel](https://hazyresearch.stanford.edu/blog/2025-09-28-tp-llama-main) targets throughput with multi-GPU tensor parallelism and heterogeneous overlap. [MPK](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.22219) compiles multi-GPU model inference into SM-level task graphs. Large-batch prefill remains a distinct, often compute-bound phase; fusion must beat strong GEMM/attention kernels. |
| **Expert-parallel MoE training** | Forward/backward expert work and communication have fine-grained overlap opportunities; deterministic ordering and precision become important. | [UniEP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.19241) reports 1.03–1.38× over its baselines on Hopper clusters. [Mixture-of-Kittens](https://github.com/cursor/mixture-of-kittens) publishes deterministic forward/backward MoE megakernels for NVL72 and says they are used in production training. The evidence is for the **MoE layer**, not whole-model training fused into one launch. |
| **Iterative stencils, sparse solvers, simulation** | Keep a short repeated step on GPU; reuse hot state and reduce host-loop dispatches. | [PERKS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.02064) evaluates persistent 2D/3D stencils and conjugate-gradient solves. [GPU RL simulation work](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.07736) fuses multiple simulation steps into one launch. These are often called *persistent* or *temporally fused* kernels rather than whole-program megakernels. |
| **Path tracing / ray workloads** | A single path program can retain path state locally and avoid wavefront queue traffic when shaders are simple and coherent. | The classic [“Megakernels Considered Harmful” paper](https://research.nvidia.com/sites/default/files/pubs/2013-07_Megakernels-Considered-Harmful/laine2013hpg_paper.pdf) found wavefront stages better for scenes with multiple complex materials because of branch divergence and register use. [OptiX engineers](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/optix-and-performance-counter-reports-in-nsight-compute/180642) also describe an OptiX launch as one kernel containing raygen/hit/miss programs. This is a distinct use of “megakernel” from LLM schedulers. |
| **Irregular graphs / dynamic neural networks** | Small, input-dependent tasks could benefit from a device scheduler and less host coordination. | [ACS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.12377) identifies this scheduling problem for deep-RL simulation and dynamic DNNs, but proposes concurrent-kernel orchestration rather than proving a universal megakernel. [NVIDIA's BFS study](https://research.nvidia.com/sites/default/files/pubs/2012-02_Scalable-GPU-Graph/ppo213s-merrill.pdf) found separate kernels faster than a fused version for most tested graphs. Treat this as workload-dependent, not a blanket target. |

Diffusion/image/video generation is a **candidate**, particularly if a small denoiser or iterative controller is launch-bound, but the sources above do not establish a general whole-model diffusion megakernel advantage. Similarly, a single large GEMM, attention kernel, or fused activation is valuable kernel engineering but does not by itself demonstrate whole-workload megakernel benefit. The practical question is whether a specific measured pipeline has *cross-stage bubbles* that fusion can remove without sacrificing occupancy or specialized math throughput. [Hazy's low-latency analysis](https://hazyresearch.stanford.edu/blog/2025-05-27-no-bubbles), [CUDA Graphs](https://docs.nvidia.com/cuda/cuda-programming-guide/04-special-topics/cuda-graphs.html), [Laine et al.](https://research.nvidia.com/sites/default/files/pubs/2013-07_Megakernels-Considered-Harmful/laine2013hpg_paper.pdf).

## Implementation support matrix (models, batch, GPU, parallelism)

**How to read this table.** A model or batch listed here is **demonstrated in the linked artifact or paper**, not a promise that every checkpoint in its family works. `B` means concurrent decode sequences/requests unless explicitly marked **tokens/rank** (an MoE-layer input, not a serving batch). `TP` is tensor parallelism; `EP` is expert parallelism; `SP` is sequence parallelism; `DP` is data parallelism. `NR` means the source does not state a reliable value. “One decode step” and “whole generation in one launch” are different boundaries. GPUs in an evaluation are not necessarily the only GPUs a source can compile for. Paper-only or binary-only artifacts are identified so the matrix does not imply a reproducible open-source implementation.

**Practical readout.** Public, source-available **whole-model + multi-GPU** examples with explicit evaluated batch/GPU data are MPK and Triton-distributed's Qwen3 demo; Hazy's Llama-70B branch is the high-batch research counterpart. Cohere is the most concrete public **single-H100 serving** implementation, but only for one checkpoint and B≤8. TileRT is broader at very large MoE checkpoints and now has an AMD MI350X/MI355X backend, but its optimized backend is binary-distributed. FlashInfer MegaMoE, FlashMoE, MonoMoE and Mixture-of-Kittens are **layer** implementations; they cannot be substituted for a complete decoding engine. [MPK artifact](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.22219), [Triton-distributed benchmark](https://github.com/ByteDance-Seed/Triton-distributed/blob/main/docs/getting-started/megakernel/megakernel.md), [Cohere README](https://github.com/cohere-ai/cohere-megakernel), [TileRT README](https://github.com/tile-ai/TileRT), [MegaMoE](https://flashinfer.ai/2026/09/22/mega-moe.html).

**Local reproduction update (2026-09-29).** On B200, AutoMegaKernel's real SmolLM2-135M CUDA VM matched Hugging Face for a two-token smoke, and the published model-as-a-kernel v1 artifact passed whole-model and B≤8 tests. The seven-repo follow-up also passed Mirage's standalone MoE-task reference checks, Triton-distributed's bundled LittleKernel SM100 GEMMs (9/9), FlashInfer's single-rank BF16 and W4A4 MegaMoE references, and a FlashFormer-generated copy kernel. Those are **components**, not the projects' whole-model or distributed results. B200-adapted MegaQwen matched only **19/20** Qwen3-0.6B reference tokens even though its upstream verifier printed “PASS” after comparing the first word. Both MegaQwen and qwen_megakernel build RoPE tables with theta `10,000` while the cached official Qwen3-0.6B config says `1,000,000`; that mismatch warrants investigation before claiming full-checkpoint parity. B200-adapted Luce failed its own prefill/decode check. These off-target GPU results do not reproduce or refute authors' reported RTX 3090/5090 numbers; see [exact commands and caveats](REPRODUCTIONS.md).

**Excluded / uncertain claims.** [Luminal](https://github.com/luminal-ai/luminal) publicly demonstrates Q8 Llama-3-8B on H100 as an inference compiler, but its README does not tie that benchmark to a specific one-launch megakernel mode or state the batch, so it is not counted as a demonstrated megakernel configuration. [MaruthiV's Qwen3-0.6B README](https://github.com/MaruthiV/megakernel) lists head dimension 64 and MLP intermediate size 2,816, whereas the **compiled** `src/config.cuh` uses the expected 128 and 3,072. The README and host benchmark's byte estimates are stale, but official-checkpoint parity and performance remain unverified; see [the local reproduction log](REPRODUCTIONS.md). [FlashFormer](https://github.com/cheetah-lang/flashformer) paper results are included, but its public repository is not a confirmed whole-model runner. This is why “paper result,” “shape test,” “runs a checkpoint,” and “production-supported model” are not interchangeable.

## Beyond NVIDIA and text LLMs (2026-09-29 update)

**Bottom line.** This is no longer a CUDA-only idea, but the public evidence is still fragmented: TPU v7 has an open, whole-model, cross-chip LLM implementation with measured baselines; Trainium2/3 has an official open multi-layer token-generation kernel but no public end-to-end speed comparison found here; AMD has both a high-performance closed full-generation engine and open layer/other-workload kernels. Non-LLM *graphics* megakernels have a longer history than LLM ones, while full end-to-end image/video/audio/robotics models are usually **partly** fused, not literally one dispatch. TPU and Trainium are accelerators, not GPUs; they are included because the scheduling idea transfers. These are research-survey findings, not results reproduced on the local B200.

**Apple LLM status.** I found no verified open-source, checkpoint-backed **whole-model Apple-GPU megakernel** with a reproducible same-device baseline. [BaseRT's paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00501) and [code](https://github.com/basecompute/baseRT) report native Metal fusion and up to **1.56×** decode over llama.cpp on evaluated M3/M4 Pro devices, but the documented kernel-pass boundaries do not establish one whole-model dispatch, so it belongs in the adjacent *fused-kernel runtime* category. A [Mirage Metal request](https://github.com/mirage-project/mirage/issues/765) is a request, not a port. Metal documents [`threadgroup_barrier` and `simdgroup_barrier`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/apple-silicon/porting-your-metal-code-to-apple-silicon), not a built-in grid-wide barrier. Thus the demonstrated Apple designs either make each threadgroup's work independent or confine dependent stages to one group; a truly cross-group whole-model schedule would need a separately validated device-memory protocol or multiple dispatches. This is a design inference, not a claim that cross-group coordination is impossible.

**Benchmark landscape.** There is **no established cross-hardware, cross-workload benchmark of authentic megakernels** in these sources. Project benchmarks answer different questions: Inferact/Kog use tok/s, tridec uses decoder latency plus logical-error-rate/serving capacity, robotics uses per-stage and end-to-end policy latency, and rendering uses FPS/quality; the AWS Trainium kernel has no comparable public speed figure identified here. [KernelBench-Mega](https://github.com/Infatoshi/kernelbench.com/tree/master/benchmarks/mega) is a small *agent kernel-writing* benchmark rather than an accelerator leaderboard; its [devlog](https://github.com/Infatoshi/kernelbench.com/blob/master/benchmarks/mega/DEVLOG.md) explicitly records that early high-scoring CUDA-Graph submissions were **not** single-launch megakernels and added an authenticity audit. For any comparison, report **dispatch count and fusion boundary, exact model/checkpoint and input shape, B/context/steps, hardware count/topology/precision, correctness, cold compile time separately from steady-state latency, and a strong same-hardware graph/wavefront/operator baseline**. Cross-vendor headline ratios without the same checkpoint, workload, and baseline remain suggestive rather than causal.

## Implementation taxonomy: schedule, synchronization, and footprint

These are **mechanism families**, not mutually exclusive product labels: a whole-model kernel may use local barriers inside a tile, counters between tiles, and collectives between GPUs. “Instruction-cache footprint” means executable instructions the GPU fetches; it is **not** the same as task descriptors, shared-memory allocation, or weight-cache locality. The last column names an observed mechanism or a risk, **not** an unmeasured instruction-cache speedup.

| Family and representative implementations | Work assignment / dependency ordering | Code and resource-footprint strategy; principal risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Static per-SM instruction stream:** [Hazy Llama-1B](https://hazyresearch.stanford.edu/blog/2025-05-27-no-bubbles), [ForgeMegakernel](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12379) | Host/compiler builds a fine-grained instruction schedule; resident SM workers interpret it. Ring/semaphore or dependency counters release an instruction when producers finish, avoiding full-grid barriers on every stage. | Reuse a fixed set of device instruction handlers across layers instead of generating one distinct copy per task. This is a **code-reuse rationale**, not a published I-cache miss reduction. Interpretation and dispatch branches still cost cycles. |
| **Host-built tiled task list:** [Cohere](https://github.com/cohere-ai/cohere-megakernel), [Hazy TP Llama-70B](https://hazyresearch.stanford.edu/blog/2025-09-28-tp-llama-main) | Cohere launches roughly one persistent CTA/SM and orders cross-SM tiles with device-memory atomic completion counters; its prefill remains separate. Hazy TP uses an atomic global work queue/work stealing and overlaps compute, memory, and peer-GPU communication. Neither requires a full-grid barrier for every dependency. | Tiled device-function repertoire and a compact task stream amortize scheduling code, while specialized handlers retain fast math. Counters, queues, branch dispatch, and the maximum register demand can erode gains; no published I-cache attribution identified here. |
| **Compiler-generated event/task DAG:** [Mirage MPK](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.22219), [Fleet](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15379) | MPK decomposes operators to SM tasks and uses decentralized scheduler/worker queues; completion events enqueue ready successors. Fleet extends this to chiplet-affine workers: local L2 counters for intra-chiplet work, then **one fenced global signal per chiplet** for cross-chiplet dependencies. | MPK fuses events **37–118×** and linearizes successor lists **4.4–15×** in its tests, shrinking **synchronization metadata**, not measured instruction bytes. Paged shared memory changes per task; compiled per-thread registers remain fixed at the maximum among task types. Fleet improves **data-L2** locality, which is distinct from I-cache. |
| **Resident bulk-synchronous grid:** [qwen_megakernel](https://github.com/AlpinDale/qwen_megakernel), [Luce](https://github.com/PixelML/luce-megakernel), this repo's [TIRx Qwen3](sandbox/tirx/megakernel_qwen3.py), [Kog AMD](https://blog.kog.ai/building-a-single-kernel-latency-optimized-llm-inference-engine-on-amd-mi300x-gpus/) | Fixed resident blocks execute stages/layers in order, using a grid-wide barrier or device-memory atomic counter/sense. The locally cloned qwen_megakernel additionally uses KV/attention readiness flags to replace selected full barriers; Kog reports polling-based synchronization. Grid residency is a correctness requirement for spin barriers. | A runtime layer/token loop reuses stage code rather than fully unrolling the model, which can constrain static code growth. Global barriers idle workers and consume time; Kog reports ~35% barrier time. Large register/shared-memory demand can reduce residency. No source here proves an I-cache improvement from the loop. |
| **Distributed single-layer dataflow:** [FlashMoE](https://github.com/osayamenja/FlashMoE), [FlashInfer MegaMoE](https://flashinfer.ai/2026/09/22/mega-moe.html), [Weave](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21483), [Mixture-of-Kittens](https://github.com/cursor/mixture-of-kittens) | Route/quantize, expert compute, transfer, and combine are tiled inside one **MoE-layer** execution; device-side NVSHMEM/symmetric-memory signaling or specialized communication workers overlap ranks. Each scheme has its own memory-order and collective-launch contract. | Specialize to one layer and its quantization/architecture rather than carry whole-model code. Fusion saves launch/intermediate traffic but can raise register/shared-memory pressure; public claims are mainly throughput/latency, not I-cache counter measurements. |
| **Single-device operator/layer fusion:** [MonoMoE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04244), [MLRift](https://github.com/Heniokhos-Systems/MLRift/blob/main/docs/SLICE4_MEGAKERNEL_DESIGN.md), [Enigma-DSL example](https://github.com/Klyne-org/Enigma-DSL/blob/main/examples/qwen_megakernel.py), [GR00T action head](https://huggingface.co/changh95/GR00T-N1.6-3B-p150/blame/main/SERVING.md), [RLDX action head](https://huggingface.co/changh95/RLDX-1-PT-IMG-p150) | Stages of **one operator, layer, block, or denoising action head** execute inside one dispatch; dependencies use local ordering, barriers, or a fixed persistent worker schedule according to the backend. The full model or modality pipeline still crosses dispatch boundaries. | A smaller fusion boundary caps the number of kernels/task handlers that must coexist in one program. It trades away some possible cross-layer launch savings for simpler residency and compilation. Specific I-cache behavior is unreported. |
| **Sequential core program plus explicit transfers:** [Inferact TPU Pallas](https://inferact.ai/blog/tpu-megakernels), [AWS Trainium NKI](https://awsdocs-neuron.readthedocs-hosted.com/en/latest/nki/library/api/transformer-tkg.html) | TPU uses one grid-less program: local stage ordering is program order, async HBM↔VMEM copies have explicit waits, and cross-chip work uses collectives. NKI loops over layers in one invocation with explicit inter-layer all-reduce. CUDA-style SM grid barriers are not the organizing abstraction. | TPU `run_scoped` controls the lifetime of its **64 MiB/TensorCore VMEM** scratch; NKI uses SBUF/buffer management. These are on-chip data-footprint controls, not measurements of instruction-cache occupancy. |
| **Independent item/path or single-threadgroup temporal fusion:** [Skinny](https://github.com/bilgili/skinny/blob/main/docs/Megakernel.md), [tridec](https://github.com/bledden/tridec/blob/master/README.md), [AppleSiliconFFT](https://github.com/aminems/AppleSiliconFFT), [PERKS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.02064) | A pixel/path, quantum shot, FFT line, or local time tile carries its own dependent sequence; sync is SIMD/threadgroup-local or unnecessary between independent items. Global coupling in a stencil/solver still requires an appropriate grid protocol or a boundary. | State can remain in registers/threadgroup memory, but a monolithic material/path program can grow large and diverge. Skinny explicitly reports a Metal compiler kernel-size limit and mitigates watchdog risk by tiling heavy frames into multiple launches; [classic wavefront path tracing](https://research.nvidia.com/publication/2013-07_megakernels-considered-harmful-wavefront-path-tracing-gpus) shows why splitting can win. |
| **Static offline schedule / generated specialization:** [Ada-MK](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.11581), [FlashFormer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22758), [DITRON](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.02953) | Offline DAG search, Python/Cheetah generation, or Triton task tiling specializes one model/shape; dependencies are encoded by the generated schedule and its task/barrier primitives rather than a general runtime graph. | Ada-MK explicitly removes runtime branching and reports 50% lower **peak shared memory** via K-splitting. That does not establish smaller machine code; excessive specialization/unrolling could enlarge it. Per-system binary size and I-cache misses need direct measurement. |
| **Published scheduling abstraction, opaque low-level engine:** [TileRT](https://github.com/tile-ai/TileRT) | Public description is multi-GPU tile-task persistent decode, but optimized CUDA/ROCm model backends are distributed as binary wheels. | Its public artifact is insufficient to assign a precise barrier protocol, task-handler layout, or I-cache mitigation. Do not infer any of those from benchmark numbers. |

**Where the instruction-cache question stands.** The sources above substantiate *code-size hazards* (Skinny's Metal compile limit, [wavefront-vs-megakernel path tracing](https://research.nvidia.com/publication/2013-07_megakernels-considered-harmful-wavefront-path-tracing-gpus)) and *strategies plausibly limiting static code duplication* (layer loops, reusable handlers, task descriptors). They do **not** provide a comparable set of compiled-binary sizes, I-cache miss rates, or front-end stall counters for current LLM megakernels. MPK's **4.4–15× task-metadata reduction** and Fleet's **data-L2 hit-rate gains** must not be presented as I-cache gains. A clean experiment would compare a looped/interpreted version with a fully unrolled/specialized version on identical work and hardware, recording executable code size, occupancy/registers, branch behavior, front-end stalls/I-cache misses where exposed, and steady-state latency. [MPK measurements](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.22219), [Fleet measurements](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15379), [Nsight Compute profiling guide](https://docs.nvidia.com/nsight-compute/ProfilingGuide/).

## AI-agent approaches to writing megakernels (2026-09-29 update)

**What counts.** An *agentic* approach lets a coding model choose and revise source code, a schedule IR, or tuning parameters using compiler, correctness, profiler, and latency feedback. This differs from an ordinary compiler that deterministically lowers a model graph, or an autotuner that exhaustively tries a fixed knob set. It also differs from “agentic workload” inference: serving a coding agent does not imply that an agent wrote the GPU kernel. The direct whole-model evidence is currently concentrated on NVIDIA CUDA decode; the broader agent-kernel ecosystem has useful methods but mostly evaluates **individual operators**, not genuine whole-model megakernels. [ForgeMegakernel](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.12379), [AutoMegaKernel](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09682), [KernelBench-Mega specification](https://github.com/Infatoshi/kernelbench.com/blob/master/benchmarks/mega/SPEC.md).

| Project / agent's edit surface | Independent gate and search loop | Demonstrated result and availability |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **[ForgeMegakernel](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.12379): agent edits a per-model CUDA decode implementation.** Ten ordered, model-independent milestones progress from operator kernels to one persistent launch, per-SM instruction streams, dependency counters in place of grid barriers, and pooled shared memory. Each round starts a fresh coding-agent context; a second agent audits passed rounds. | A harness-owned **mid-state oracle** checks per-layer KV rows, logits against float64/production references, precision contracts, poisoned-KV and multi-lane cases, one-launch structure, memory-bandwidth utilization (MBU), and measured H100 profiler counters. The agent cannot set its own pass threshold. | Paper reports **14 model/batch/context cells**, eight model families (0.6B–13B), **B=1–16**, context 96–4096, **50.5–85.9% MBU**, and **1.21× geometric-mean speedup vs SGLang 0.5.18** on H100. Median campaign: **47 rounds, 14.7 agent-hours, ~$177** in reported model charges. A second Codex-agent run reached 35.7% MBU on one Qwen3-0.6B cell. Paper/experiment description available; no corresponding public buildable Forge code was identified in this search. [Method and costs](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.12379). |
| **[AutoMegaKernel (AMK)](https://github.com/RightNow-AI/AutoMegaKernel): agent edits typed schedule configuration and isolated micro-kernels, not its trusted scheduler/validator.** A Hugging Face Llama-family model lowers to per-SM task queues; the same source retargets several NVIDIA architectures. This is one B=1 short-context, single-GPU campaign, not evidence about the approach's general ceiling. |
| **[KernelBench-Mega](https://github.com/Infatoshi/kernelbench.com/blob/master/benchmarks/mega/SPEC.md): agents submit a whole-block fused solution to a fixed benchmark**, rather than compiling arbitrary LLMs. The current deck has **one** Kimi-Linear W4A16 decode block task; a formerly listed RL/PPO task was removed. | Immutable reference/shape/tolerance files, multiple seeds, disposable agent workspace, and a pre-publication source-authenticity audit. The [devlog](https://github.com/Infatoshi/kernelbench.com/blob/master/benchmarks/mega/DEVLOG.md) found that **none of 36 earlier decode submissions were genuine single-launch megakernels**: high scores came from multi-kernel CUDA Graph replay. | **[Houmao agentic kernel optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14560): general-purpose coding agents generate a fused MoE operator**, plus other FlashInfer-Bench operators, from PyTorch specs and hardware skills. | Multi-agent generate/debug/profile loop with a fixed correctness-gated contest harness; humans orchestrate but reportedly do not edit generated kernel code. | Paper reports a **1.71× Fused-MoE speedup over the FlashInfer baseline on B200** in the official contest and roughly **1.9 billion agent tokens** across its study. **Transferable but not direct megakernel results.** [PyTorch KernelAgent](https://pytorch.org/blog/kernelagent-hardware-guided-gpu-kernel-optimization-via-multi-agent-orchestration/) feeds Nsight-style hardware signals into a multi-agent Triton loop; [AMD GEAK v4](https://www.amd.com/en/developer/resources/technical-articles/2026/geak-v4.html) applies a similar hypothesis/edit/test/profile process on Instinct GPUs; [Meta KernelEvolve](https://engineering.fb.com/2026/04/02/developer-tools/kernelevolve-how-metas-ranking-engineer-agent-optimizes-ai-infrastructure/) treats heterogeneous kernel engineering as search. [MaxKernel](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.04523) offers human-in-the-loop, autonomous, and graph-search modes for TPU Pallas kernels, with a **frozen test suite**; its **1.58× geometric-mean speedup over JAX/XLA** on 50 JaxBench tasks is the *best of five parallel agent trajectories per task*, with regressions floored at 1× in the aggregate metric. None of those cited results demonstrates an agent-generated *whole-model* AMD, Apple, or TPU megakernel; importing their workflow is an engineering proposal, not a measured portability result.

**What a credible agent harness needs.** The strongest designs converge on a loop: freeze the workload and numerical contract; constrain what the agent may edit; compile and reject invalid synchronization **before** running a potentially hanging persistent grid; check independent intermediate states and long-rollout outputs; verify the *actual timed dispatch count*; profile occupancy, barriers, memory traffic and code size; then compare a correct candidate against an incumbent and a same-hardware graph/serving baseline, keeping only measured improvements. Explicitly record model/checkpoint, B/context, precision, GPU, compile time, agent trials/tokens/cost, and failed variants. These are recommendations inferred from [Forge's independent oracle](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.12379), [AMK's schedule gate](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09682), [MaxKernel's frozen tests](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.04523), and [KernelBench-Mega's authenticity failure](https://github.com/Infatoshi/kernelbench.com/blob/master/benchmarks/mega/DEVLOG.md), not a claim that one current system implements every safeguard.

**For this repository.** The natural experimental target is one fixed B=1 Qwen3 cell in [sandbox/tirx](sandbox/tirx/): keep [the PyTorch reference](sandbox/tirx/qwen3_ref.py) and [verifier](sandbox/tirx/verify_megakernel.py) outside an agent's edit surface, give the agent one schedule/geometry change at a time, and preserve the existing [benchmark](sandbox/tirx/bench_megakernel.py) as an independent gate. Add intermediate-state taps and an explicit dispatch-count/occupancy check before claiming a Forge- or AMK-style autonomous result. This is a proposed workflow, **not yet an implemented agent harness or a reproduced speedup**.

## Hardware: what is actually required

**Functional minimum.** A GPU and toolchain that can execute the chosen kernel, sufficient device memory for its resident data, and a correct way to order producer/consumer stages. No tensor-core generation, NVLink, or HBM is inherent to the megakernel concept: [Luce's RTX 3090](https://github.com/PixelML/luce-megakernel) and [Kog's AMD MI300X](https://blog.kog.ai/building-a-single-kernel-latency-optimized-llm-inference-engine-on-amd-mi300x-gpus/) use different architectures. For LLMs, budget **weights + KV/recurrent state + activations/scratch + communication workspace + framework overhead**; model parameter count alone does not determine fit. Low-batch decode is usually sensitive to usable memory bandwidth, while larger-batch GEMMs and prefill can become compute-bound. [Hazy single-GPU analysis](https://hazyresearch.stanford.edu/blog/2025-05-27-no-bubbles), [Hazy tensor-parallel analysis](https://hazyresearch.stanford.edu/blog/2025-09-28-tp-llama-main).

| --- | --- | --- |
| **NVIDIA RTX 3090 / Ampere** | Consumer-GPU whole-model decode is possible; Luce reports ~1.5 GB VRAM for its BF16 0.8B model and requires CUDA 12+. | [Luce requirements](https://github.com/PixelML/luce-megakernel). A specific kernel can still require more memory or newer instructions. |

These requirements are **package requirements**, not properties of all megakernels.

**Residency and synchronization are hard constraints.** A hand-rolled full-grid spin barrier can deadlock if waiting blocks consume all execution slots before other blocks can arrive. Size the persistent grid against measured *resident blocks per SM* after accounting for registers, threads, and shared memory; use a supported cooperative launch for CUDA/HIP grid synchronization or a carefully designed dependency-counter protocol. CUDA block-cluster shared memory requires compute capability 9.0+, and Blackwell cluster launch control requires 10.0; neither is a prerequisite for ordinary megakernels. [CUDA cooperative groups](https://docs.nvidia.com/cuda/cuda-programming-guide/04-special-topics/cooperative-groups.html), [CUDA resource limits](https://docs.nvidia.com/cuda/cuda-programming-guide/02-basics/writing-cuda-kernels.html), [HIP cooperative groups](https://rocm.docs.amd.com/projects/HIP/en/docs-7.2.1/how-to/hip_runtime_api/cooperative_groups.html), [CUDA cluster launch control](https://docs.nvidia.com/cuda/cuda-programming-guide/04-special-topics/cluster-launch-control.html). For distributed kernels, NVSHMEM provides device-initiated puts/gets/atomics and has specific collective-launch rules; mere access to two GPUs is insufficient for a particular NVSHMEM implementation. [NVSHMEM programming guide](https://docs.nvidia.com/nvshmem/api/latest/using.html), [NVSHMEM collective-launch rules](https://docs.nvidia.com/nvshmem/api/latest/api/overview.html).

## How to decide whether to build one

1. **Define the dispatch boundary:** one operator, one MoE layer, one model decode step, or an entire token loop. Published “megakernel” speedups use all four, so compare equivalent work. [MonoMoE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04244), [Cohere](https://github.com/cohere-ai/cohere-megakernel), [local Qwen3](sandbox/tirx/megakernel_qwen3.py).
2. **Measure the best simpler baseline:** tuned operator kernels with CUDA/HIP Graphs and relevant compiler/serving settings. A graph already reduces CPU launch overhead; a megakernel must win on remaining device-side gaps, fine-grained dependency overlap, intermediate traffic, or communication. [CUDA Graphs](https://docs.nvidia.com/cuda/cuda-programming-guide/04-special-topics/cuda-graphs.html), [Hazy gap analysis](https://hazyresearch.stanford.edu/blog/2025-05-27-no-bubbles).
3. **Check the residency budget and critical path:** compile-time registers and shared memory, active blocks/SM, weight/KV bytes, branch divergence, barrier count/latency, and interconnect traffic. A large kernel can spill registers or strand lanes; wavefront/split kernels may then win. [CUDA occupancy discussion](https://docs.nvidia.com/cuda/cuda-programming-guide/02-basics/writing-cuda-kernels.html), [path-tracing counterexample](https://research.nvidia.com/sites/default/files/pubs/2013-07_Megakernels-Considered-Harmful/laine2013hpg_paper.pdf).
4. **Verify every intermediate dependency and numerical contract:** test varying batch/context/expert balance, stage outputs, repeated runs, and the intended precision/order of reductions. In-kernel synchronization replaces implicit launch-boundary ordering and can fail silently. [ForgeMegakernel mid-state oracle](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12379), [Compute Sanitizer](https://docs.nvidia.com/compute-sanitizer/ComputeSanitizer/index.html), [UniEP deterministic ordering](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.19241).

**Repository-specific starting point.** This repo already has TIRx whole-token-loop examples for [dense Qwen3](sandbox/tirx/megakernel_qwen3.py), [generic Qwen3 geometry](sandbox/tirx/megakernel_llm.py), [Qwen3-MoE](sandbox/tirx/megakernel_moe.py), and [GPT-OSS](sandbox/tirx/megakernel_gptoss.py), with adjacent [verification](sandbox/tirx/verify_megakernel.py) and [benchmarks](sandbox/tirx/bench_megakernel.py). Those are an implementation case study, not evidence that the same schedule or B200-specific geometry transfers unchanged to another GPU.

### Scope and evidence notes

This survey searched whole-model, persistent, distributed-MoE, rendering, iterative-science, and simulation work; vendor hardware/programming documentation; and project repositories. It is broad but cannot be literally exhaustive across unpublished/proprietary kernels or fast-moving branches. Preprints and project README benchmarks are labeled as author-reported and should be reproduced before procurement or performance commitments. The most important open question for any new workload is empirical: whether the gain from removing boundaries exceeds added synchronization, code size, register pressure, and scheduling complexity.
